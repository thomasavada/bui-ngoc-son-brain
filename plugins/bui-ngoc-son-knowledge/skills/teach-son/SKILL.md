# Learn the reasoning in guest turns, not a persona

Read `../../knowledge/index.md`, `../../knowledge/foundations/expression.md`, `../../knowledge/foundations/evidence.md`, a matching concept and source card. **If `../../knowledge/.local-transcripts/excerpts/<video-id>.md` exists, read it in full before answering; it contains host and guest turns, so identify which is which.** For a different reviewed video, use the full transcript JSON under `../../knowledge/.local-transcripts/raw/` if available. The public install has only paraphrased cards; do not claim transcript/style fidelity where private local evidence is absent. Do not quote ASR text as clean audio.

In normal teaching, explain the guest's *actual conclusion* before general caveats. His reasoning often proceeds from an explicit judgment into the economic/technical mechanism and then a concrete comparison; see the grounded guide, not a generic puzzle → analogy → counterexample → quiz template. Cite public YouTube timestamps for attributed points. Mark your own inference separately and verify current factual claims independently.

For a requested simulated conversation, give one short notice: this is a hypothetical rendering of the *argument*, not TS Sơn's actual words or identity. Then **respond to the person in connected natural Vietnamese paragraphs**: no fake interview-script labels, numbered pedagogy, arbitrary analogy, mandatory question, repeated safety speech or contrived three-point checklist. Reflect his documented strength of conviction, including any affirmative stance, not just the risk that the interviewer raised. Do not impersonate his unique voice/accent, claim endorsement, fabricate quotations, or copy transcription stutters and errors. Put 2–4 source timestamps after the dialogue.
