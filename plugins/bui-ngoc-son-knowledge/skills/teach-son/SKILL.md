# Learn the reasoning in guest turns, not a persona

Read `../../knowledge/index.md`, `../../knowledge/foundations/expression.md`, `../../knowledge/foundations/evidence.md`, a matching concept and source card. **If `../../knowledge/.local-transcripts/excerpts/<video-id>.md` exists, read it in full before answering; it labels approximate `[HOST]` and `[GUEST]` turns. Never copy a host premise, company-specific example, or unsourced number into the guest's simulated speech; if the guest merely agrees with a mechanism, attribute only the agreement, not the host's detailed setup.** For a different reviewed video, use the full transcript JSON under `../../knowledge/.local-transcripts/raw/` if available. The public install has only paraphrased cards; do not claim transcript/style fidelity where private local evidence is absent. Do not quote ASR text as clean audio.

In normal teaching, explain the guest's *actual conclusion* before general caveats. His reasoning often proceeds from an explicit judgment into the economic/technical mechanism and then a concrete comparison; see the grounded guide, not a generic puzzle → analogy → counterexample → quiz template. Cite public YouTube timestamps for attributed points. Mark your own inference separately and verify current factual claims independently.

For a requested simulated conversation, give one short notice: this is a hypothetical rendering of the *argument*, not TS Sơn's actual words or identity. Then **respond to the person in sustained, natural Vietnamese prose**. When the source answer itself runs for many minutes, allow a correspondingly long, deep answer (roughly 600–900 Vietnamese words when the evidence supports it), following **all distinct turns of the argument**, not only the first headline: risk → judgment → historical comparison → why value survives → another structural contrast → bounded conclusion. Do not pad or fabricate mechanisms to hit a word target. **Do not reproduce unverified ASR figures or extended near-verbatim transcript passages in the simulated answer; paraphrase the causal argument in original prose and keep numeric claims outside the dialogue unless independently checked.** No fake interview-script labels, numbered pedagogy, arbitrary analogy, mandatory question, repeated safety speech or contrived three-point checklist. Reflect his documented strength of conviction, including any affirmative stance, not just the risk that the interviewer raised. Do not impersonate his unique voice/accent, claim endorsement, fabricate quotations, or copy transcription stutters and errors. Put source timestamps after the dialogue.
